# Strategic Principles Extracted From the Provided Materials

> Source-material caveat: no logo file, Grüns transcript, AG1 transcript, or product formula sheet was actually present in the build session. The principles below are extracted from the brief's own explicit descriptions of those sources, not from independent transcript review. Treat this document as the interpretation layer between "what the brief said the videos concluded" and "what the store actually does about it."

| # | Principle (source) | What it means | How it's implemented in BullFuel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category-blend positioning (Grüns) | Grüns succeeded by sitting between two familiar categories (greens powder + gummy vitamin) rather than inventing a category no one recognizes. | BullFuel sits between "adult gummy supplement" and "premium male vitality/performance." The line "Premium male vitality support in a daily gummy format" appears above the fold on the homepage hero, the DR landing page hero, and the product offer page — it's the one-sentence categorization every visitor needs before anything else. |
| 2 | Premium pricing supports the whole funnel (Grüns) | A too-cheap price can't fund paid acquisition, affiliate commission, or subscription discounting, and it signals low quality. | Reference one-time price is set at a real, defensible number (see `05-pricing-framework.md`), not a race-to-the-bottom price. Subscription discount is presented as a reward for commitment, not the "real" price with a fake markup above it. |
| 3 | Subscription-first, fully disclosed (Grüns) | LTV, not first-order margin, is the business model — but only works long-term if customers trust the mechanics. | Every purchase module shows: first-order price, recurring price, frequency, next-charge timing, skip option, cancel option — inline, not in a modal or footnote. Subscription is the visually recommended tab, never the only readable one. |
| 4 | Proof as a conversion engine, never fabricated (Grüns) | Trust claims must be real; a supplement brand's downside for faking proof is severe (legally and reputationally). | Dedicated, clearly labeled `[PLACEHOLDER — pending real data]` blocks for reviews, UGC, third-party testing, GMP/manufacturing statements, and ingredient sourcing. Problem-proof statistics (male fatigue/stress prevalence) are kept separate from product-proof and require a cited, credible external source before publishing — see `07-proof-acquisition-plan.md`. |
| 5 | One flagship offer only (AG1) | AG1 built and defended a single-SKU funnel for years; catalogue browsing kills conversion on a new brand. | Homepage, DR landing page, and product-offer page show exactly one product: BullFuel Male Vitality Gummies. Any future SKU appears only in the cart drawer, post-purchase upsell, or retention email — never on the primary decision surfaces. |
| 6 | Reduce decision overload (AG1) | The only choice a visitor should make is quantity/commitment level, not product, flavor, or formula. | Purchase module = exactly 4 choices: 1 bottle / 3 bottles / 6 bottles / Subscribe & Save. No flavor selector, no formula variants at launch. |
| 7 | Ethical price anchoring (AG1) | Anchoring works, but only if the reference price is real and the discount math is honest. | Per-bottle price drops step-wise from 1→3→6→Subscribe, with real total prices and real per-bottle math shown — see the pricing table. No inflated "you'd pay $XXX elsewhere" claims. |
| 8 | Identity-based, non-embarrassing tier naming (AG1) | Naming can reinforce identity without resorting to "Alpha Male" style copy that undermines the premium positioning. | Tiers are named Start the Routine / Build Consistency / Commit to the Protocol / Stay Fuelled — confidence and consistency framing, no exaggerated masculinity copy. |
| 9 | Remove distractions after the buy click (AG1) | Once someone has decided to look at purchasing, every extra nav link is a leak in the funnel. | DR landing page and product-offer page use a stripped header (logo + cart only, no full nav). Sticky mobile "Choose Your Supply" bar keeps the CTA present at all times without adding new links. |
| 10 | Upsells belong after the primary decision (AG1) | Cross-selling before the first purchase decision dilutes focus; it belongs in the cart, post-purchase, or retention flows. | See `10-cart-checkout-upsells.md` — no upsell exists anywhere above the Add to Cart button on any of the three funnel surfaces. |
| 11 | Packaging built for advertising performance (AG1) | A product needs to be legible as a thumbnail on Meta/TikTok, not just on a shelf. | Mockup spec: matte-black jar, large red bull mark, minimal front-label copy, red/dark-red gummies for contrast — written into `13-product-mockup-prompts.md` and the CSS color system. |
| 12 | Welcome offer as a strategic hook, not a gimmick (AG1) | AG1's Welcome Kit role was to make the *first* commitment feel like more value than the price, without becoming unsustainable. | First-subscription-order discount + one realistic physical bonus (travel case) + one digital bonus (Performance Guide PDF), full value and terms disclosed — see `06-welcome-offer.md`. |
| 13 | Three connected funnel surfaces sharing one pricing source of truth | Brand awareness, cold-traffic conversion, and decision-making are different jobs and need different page structures — but must never disagree on price. | Homepage (trust-building), DR landing page (cold-traffic conversion), Product Offer page (decision-making) all render the same `purchase-module.liquid` snippet, driven by the same product/variant data — there is exactly one place pricing logic lives. |
